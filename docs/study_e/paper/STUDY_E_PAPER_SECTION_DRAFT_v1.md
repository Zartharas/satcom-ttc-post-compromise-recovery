# Part II (Study E): Loss, restart and contact-window effects on SDLS key-activation and recovery exchanges
DRAFT v1, 2026-11-19. Numbers are from CONFIRMATORY_FINDINGS_v1 (tags study-e-prereg-v1 and study-e-analysis-script-v1). Bracketed items need the author's decision or a citation check. Part I (Studies A-D) is frozen and is not reinterpreted here.

## II.1 Question and scope
Standards-track CCSDS documents define SDLS key activation and verification but leave recovery after disruption outside their scope [cite CCSDS 355.1-B-1; 354.0-M-1 s3.1.2 - author to confirm section]. SDLS-DR [Ledin et al., RISC 2026 - confirm reference] specifies state persistence and Recovery-association re-provisioning but reports no evaluation of contact windows or loss. Study E asks: when loss, spacecraft restarts and short contact windows disrupt an automated activation-and-verification exchange, how often does it finish, diverge, or stall, and how much do persistence, retry and resumability choices change that?
Study E is a parametric model study. It is not a measurement of any spacecraft link, and it makes no claim of CCSDS conformance. Cryptography is abstracted (labels, not verified primitives).

## II.2 Model
The exchange is a six-message abstraction (PREPARE, RESPONSE, COMMIT, CONFIRM, TEST_COMMAND, STATUS_TELEMETRY) in which ground activates the new epoch on CONFIRM and the spacecraft activates on COMMIT (local activation) or on authenticated status (gated activation). K message slots per contact, a transmission budget B, a candidate lifetime of L contacts, per-message loss p (independent, or a two-state Gilbert-Elliott channel as a sensitivity probe), and a per-contact-boundary restart probability r with three persistence levels (volatile, commit, journal). Outcomes: SUCCESS, INDETERMINATE, EXPIRED_OLD, DIVERGED_S_AHEAD, DIVERGED_G_AHEAD. Multi-attempt runs over a horizon of H contacts use three policies (retry expired attempts only, also restart indeterminate ones, or resume verification from the step reached; the last is an assumption outside the standards and is reported as a sensitivity). After divergence, a Recovery-association re-bootstrap (R0) follows a detection delay. The skipped-key cache desynchronisation (R2) is analysed separately and is not coupled to the exchange [limitation].
All probabilities are computed exactly (rational arithmetic) by dynamic programming, cross-checked against an independent exhaustive enumerator, closed-form cases, Monte Carlo agreement, and mutation tests; the suite has 99 tests, passing natively and in Docker. A TLC reachability check on the support is included for transparency; it is same-author and the TLA+ specification does not cover the resume policy or the Gilbert-Elliott channel.

## II.3 Parameter grid and anchors
The grid is a full factorial of p in {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5}, K in {2,3,4,6}, B in {4,6,8,12}, L in {1..4}, r in {0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1}, three persistence levels and two activation modes (7,680 configurations), crossed with H in {4,8,20}, three policies and detection delay in {1,3,5} (207,360 points). p = 0.3 and 0.5 are stress points beyond clear-sky broadband loss; their upper end is comparable to the worst broadband uplink loss reported during the 2024 solar storms [CosmicDancePro, verified against the paper], and is not measured TT&C loss (Erratum 001).
No open measured TT&C uplink-loss dataset was found, so parameters are anchored to proxies, each labelled by what it is: SatNOGS scheduled observation windows from Study 8E (476 windows, 20 satellite-station pairs; downlink observation timing, median 277 s, not contacts and not loss) [CC BY-SA 4.0 attribution to SatNOGS Network]; Starlink ping bursts [Zenodo 14987305; broadband, not TT&C; mean loss 2.16%, lossy burst followed by lossy burst 13.7% of the time versus 7.8% after a clean burst]; published loss ranges [CosmicDancePro: baseline loss up to 2% and storm-time uplink loss up to 60% at one site; WetLinks Table II: mean about 1%, 22-25% of samples with loss, maxima above 80%; both checked against the PDFs on 2026-11-19]; and orbital-geometry pass durations (modeled, not measured). Gilbert-Elliott cases use a matched-stationary rule fixed before any run (good state lossless, bad state lossy, mean bad run 2 or 4 slots).

## II.4 Pre-registration and deviations
The design, hypotheses H-E1 to H-E5, grid and policy set were frozen at tag study-e-prereg-v1 (commit 93636b1) before any confirmatory run. The confirmatory runner was added afterwards under a disclosed amendment (tag study-e-analysis-script-v1, commit 6eb87f2); it implements only the frozen hypotheses, and the operationalisations of ambiguous wording were declared before the run (for example, "almost always" recoverable = recovery fraction at least 0.95). Before the run, a small test grid had revealed witnesses against H-E2b; this was disclosed in the amendment, the hypothesis was not changed, and the exploratory check added afterwards is labelled post hoc. The Starlink statistics were seen before the Gilbert-Elliott rule was written, so the anchors are not blind.

## II.5 Results
| Hypothesis | Outcome |
|---|---|
| H-E2a: SUCCESS monotone | Holds for B, L and H (no violations). Fails for K (31 of 5,760 pairs) and p (22 of 6,144); every violation has r > 0 and non-journal persistence; largest drops 0.077 and 0.013. |
| H-E2b: more durable persistence never raises ground-ahead divergence (gated) | Falsified: 288 of 2,560 comparisons, all commit-to-journal, rise up to 0.082. |
| H-E2c: journal invariant to r; volatile divergence non-decreasing in r | Journal invariance holds (0 of 1,920 violations). Volatile monotonicity fails in 447 of 1,920 pairs, with decreases up to 0.041. |
| H-E3: retry cannot absorb INDETERMINATE mass; resumable completion is the lever | Holds at all 5,520 configurations with INDETERMINATE mass: the gain from raising H from 4 to 20 is smaller than the mass, and resumable verification gains more than raising H. |
| H-E4: divergence almost always recoverable under a static Recovery SA | Median recovery fraction is about 1, but only 89.8% of points (83.2% under restart-indeterminate) reach 0.95; minimum 0.58. Detection delay matters at H = 20 mainly at p >= 0.3 (25% of points at 0.3, 61% at 0.5; 5% at 0.05), and strongly at H = 4 (range up to 0.97). |
| H-E5: bursty loss at equal stationary loss changes outcomes in no fixed direction | Supported: bursty higher in 4,664 comparisons, lower in 3,112, equal in 7,584; maximum difference 0.317. |

## II.6 Discussion
The principal engineering lesson is that completion, not repetition, is the lever: automatic retry of expired attempts adds little because the dominant failure is an exchange that ends indeterminate, and resumable (idempotent) verification closes most of that gap (H-E3), subject to its being an assumption outside the standards. The falsified hypotheses are informative rather than embarrassing: in this model, durable persistence preserves progress that non-durable schemes reset, so under tight budgets more durable attempts end in the zone where ground has activated and the spacecraft has not (H-E2b), and extra slots or fewer losses can lower success when restarts can strike mid-exchange for non-durable persistence (H-E2a). These are consequences of the model's rules and are not claims about real links; a designer should read them as a warning that "more durable" and "more reliable link" do not compose monotonically. Bursty loss can help or hurt (H-E5), so a single i.i.d. loss figure is not a safe planning assumption.

## II.7 Limitations
Abstract six-message model, not flight software, and not independently reviewed by a second person [author to decide]; parameters anchored to proxies, none a measured TT&C uplink; loss values at 0.3 and 0.5 are stress points (comparable only to extreme-storm broadband uplink loss, not to TT&C); the Gilbert-Elliott channel is a sensitivity probe, not a calibrated channel; R0 parameters and the resume policy are assumptions; R2 is not coupled to the exchange; TLA+ check is same-author and omits the resume policy and the Gilbert-Elliott channel; the TLA+ jar digest was not compared to the publisher's; SatNOGS data is first-page and not representative.
