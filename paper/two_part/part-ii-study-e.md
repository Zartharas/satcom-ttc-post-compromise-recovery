## Part II. Study E: Loss, Restart, and Contact-Window Effects on Key-Activation and Recovery Exchanges

Part I characterizes recovery behavior in deterministic fault-injection experiments. Part II asks the parametric question posed in Section 1: how often the activation-and-verification exchange finishes, diverges, or stalls under loss, restarts, and short contact windows, and how much persistence, retry, and resumability choices change that. Study E is a model study over a declared parameter grid. It does not measure any spacecraft link, makes no claim of CCSDS conformance, and abstracts cryptography to labels.

### II.1 Model

Positioning. Beyond Section 1, the Recovery security association of CCSDS 350.5-G-2 (Sections 3.5.1.2 and 4.5.6) supplies a last-resort association, not an exchange for resynchronizing the two ends [@ccsds_sdls_rationale_350_5_g_2]. CCSDS 355.1-B-1 has the Initiator activate before signaling the Recipient (Section 3.2.3.2) and defines no handling of a lost signal in the text read [@ccsds_sdls_ep_355_1_b_1]. SDLS-DR persists ratchet state at commit points and recovers from disorderly state loss over a Recovery association; its authors state that contact windows, ground-station handover, range-dependent delay, Doppler and flight hardware were not evaluated [@ledin_sdls_dr_risc2026]. Over-the-air rekeying proposals such as E-Laun address how new keys are agreed and treat loss only qualitatively in the text read [@duc_elaun_2025]; a recent preprint models post-quantum key-update transport under loss, contact time and COP-1 retransmission, but not endpoint restart or state divergence [@kim_seo_2026]. The activation exchange also resembles the atomic-commit problem of distributed databases, where two parties must change state together over an unreliable channel and neither can be certain the other has done so [@gray_notes_1978; @skeen_nonblocking_1981]. Study E examines these gaps at the level of an abstract exchange only, with no claim of CCSDS conformance.

The exchange is a six-message abstraction (PREPARE, RESPONSE, COMMIT, CONFIRM, TEST\_COMMAND, STATUS\_TELEMETRY). Ground activates the new epoch on CONFIRM; the spacecraft activates on COMMIT (local activation) or on authenticated status (gated activation). Each contact offers $K$ message slots, an attempt has a transmission budget $B$ and a candidate lifetime of $L$ contacts, each message is lost with probability $p$ (independently, or through a two-state Gilbert-Elliott channel as a sensitivity probe), and the spacecraft restarts at a contact boundary with probability $r$ under one of three persistence levels (volatile, commit, journal). Attempts end in SUCCESS, INDETERMINATE, EXPIRED\_OLD, DIVERGED\_S\_AHEAD, or DIVERGED\_G\_AHEAD. Multi-attempt runs over a horizon of $H$ contacts use three policies: retry expired attempts only, additionally restart indeterminate attempts, or resume verification from the step reached; the last is an assumption outside the standards and is reported as a sensitivity. After divergence, a Recovery-association re-bootstrap follows a detection delay.

All probabilities are computed exactly in rational arithmetic by dynamic programming. The solver is cross-checked against an independent exhaustive enumerator, closed-form cases, Monte Carlo agreement, and mutation tests, and the suite of 99 tests passes natively and in a container.

### II.2 Parameter grid and anchors

The grid is the full factorial of $p \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$, $K \in \{2,3,4,6\}$, $B \in \{4,6,8,12\}$, $L \in \{1,\dots,4\}$, $r \in \{0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1\}$, three persistence levels, and two activation modes (7,680 configurations), crossed with $H \in \{4,8,20\}$, three policies, and detection delay in $\{1,3,5\}$ contacts (207,360 points).

No open measured TT\&C uplink-loss dataset was found, so parameters are anchored to proxies, each labelled by what it is. Observation timing comes from the SatNOGS windows used in an earlier study (476 scheduled observation windows, 20 satellite-station pairs, median duration 277 s); these are scheduled downlink observation windows, not contacts and not loss [@satnogs_api_2026]. Burst-level loss behavior comes from an 11-day consumer Starlink ping dataset [@borgianni_starlink_ping]: mean packet loss is 2.16\%, and a lossy 10-second burst is followed by another lossy burst 13.7\% of the time versus 7.8\% after a clean burst. This is broadband, not TT\&C. Published broadband ranges bracket the grid: baseline loss up to 2\% and storm-time uplink loss of up to 60\% at one site for limited periods (a figure caption; the source abstract says a 60\% increase) [@basak_cosmicdancepro], and mean loss near 1\% with 22--25\% of samples showing some loss in a longitudinal dataset [@laniewski_wetlinks]. Orbital geometry gives modeled (not measured) maximum pass durations of roughly 4--17 minutes for altitudes of 400--1200 km and 5--20 degree minimum elevations. The Gilbert-Elliott cases use a matched-stationary rule fixed before any run: a lossless good state, a fully lossy bad state, and a mean bad run of 2 or 4 slots at the same stationary loss as the independent case. Values $p = 0.3$ and $0.5$ are stress points beyond clear-sky broadband loss; their upper end is comparable to the worst broadband uplink loss reported during the 2024 solar storms and is not measured TT\&C loss.

### II.3 Pre-registration and disclosures

Hypotheses H-E1 to H-E5, the grid, and the policy set were frozen before any confirmatory run in a version-controlled repository, with the frozen versions identified in Section 9.7. The confirmatory runner was added afterwards under a disclosed amendment; it implements only the frozen hypotheses, and operationalizations of ambiguous wording were declared before the run. A small test grid run before the confirmatory run had revealed witnesses against H-E2b; the hypothesis was not changed. Starlink statistics were inspected before the Gilbert-Elliott rule was written, so the anchors are not blind. An erratum records that the frozen text cited only the baseline loss from the storm-analysis paper.

**Table 3. Study E hypotheses and outcomes over the full grid (exact arithmetic).**

| Hypothesis | Outcome |
|---|---|
| H-E2a: SUCCESS monotone in $B$, $K$, $L$, $H$ and non-increasing in $p$ | Holds for $B$, $L$, $H$ (no violations). Fails for $K$ (31 of 5,760 pairs) and $p$ (22 of 6,144); every violation has $r>0$ and non-journal persistence; largest changes 0.077 and 0.013. |
| H-E2b: more durable persistence never raises ground-ahead divergence (gated) | Falsified: 288 of 2,560 comparisons, all commit-to-journal, rise up to 0.082. |
| H-E2c: journal invariant to $r$; volatile divergence non-decreasing in $r$ | Journal invariance holds (0 of 1,920 violations). Volatile monotonicity fails in 447 of 1,920 pairs, with decreases up to 0.041. |
| H-E3: retry cannot absorb INDETERMINATE mass; resumable completion is the lever | Holds at all 5,520 configurations with INDETERMINATE mass: raising $H$ from 4 to 20 gains less than that mass, and resumable verification gains more than raising $H$. |
| H-E4: divergence almost always recoverable under a static Recovery SA | Median recovery fraction is about 1, but only 89.8\% of the 6,960 points with divergence (83.2\% under restart-indeterminate) reach the declared 0.95 threshold; minimum 0.58. Detection delay matters at $H=20$ mainly for $p\geq 0.3$ (25\% of points at 0.3, 61\% at 0.5; 5\% at 0.05) and strongly at $H=4$ (range up to 0.97). |
| H-E5: bursty loss at equal stationary loss changes outcomes in no fixed direction | Supported: bursty higher in 4,664 comparisons, lower in 3,112, equal in 7,584; maximum difference 0.317. |

### II.4 Interpretation

Retrying expired attempts adds little because the dominant failure is an exchange that ends indeterminate, and resumable verification closes most of that gap, as an assumption outside the standards. The falsified hypotheses show that more durable persistence and more reliable links do not compose monotonically: durable persistence preserves progress that non-durable schemes reset, so under tight budgets more attempts end with ground activated and the spacecraft not, and extra slots can lower success when restarts strike mid-exchange. These are consequences of the model's rules, not claims about real links, and some, such as the restart invariance of journaled persistence, hold by design; the exact computation measures their extent. Bursty loss can help or hurt, so a single independent-loss figure is not a safe planning assumption.

### II.5 Limitations specific to Study E

The six-message abstraction is not flight software and has not been independently reviewed by a second person. Parameters are anchored to proxies, none a measured TT\&C uplink. The Gilbert-Elliott channel is an uncalibrated sensitivity probe. Recovery-association and resume-policy parameters are assumptions. Skipped-key desynchronization is not coupled to the exchange. The TLA+ check is same-author, omits the resume policy and the Gilbert-Elliott channel, and its tool jar digest was not compared to the publisher's. SatNOGS records are first-page only and not representative.
