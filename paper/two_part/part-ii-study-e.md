## Part II. Study E: Loss, Restart, and Contact-Window Effects on Key-Activation and Recovery Exchanges

Part I characterizes recovery behavior in deterministic fault-injection experiments. Part II asks a complementary parametric question: when message loss, spacecraft restarts, and short contact windows disrupt an automated activation-and-verification exchange, how often does the exchange finish, diverge, or stall, and how much do persistence, retry, and resumability choices change that? Study E is a model study over a declared parameter grid. It does not measure any spacecraft link, makes no claim of CCSDS conformance, and abstracts cryptography to labels.

### II.1 Model

Positioning. CCSDS 355.1-B-1 specifies key activation and key verification as separate procedures [@ccsds_sdls_ep_355_1_b_1]; a keyword search of its text found no recovery or resynchronisation-after-loss procedure. CCSDS 354.0-M-1 states that the specification of procedures for protecting session keys and authenticating onboard crypto units, which it says includes recovery procedures, is outside its scope (Section 3.1.2.1, note) [@ccsds_key_mgmt_354_0_m_1]. SDLS-DR persists ratchet state at commit points and recovers from disorderly state loss over a Recovery association; its authors state that contact windows, ground-station handover, range-dependent delay, Doppler and flight hardware were not evaluated [@ledin_sdls_dr_risc2026]. Study E examines that gap at the level of an abstract exchange only, with no claim of CCSDS conformance.

The exchange is a six-message abstraction (PREPARE, RESPONSE, COMMIT, CONFIRM, TEST\_COMMAND, STATUS\_TELEMETRY). Ground activates the new epoch on CONFIRM; the spacecraft activates on COMMIT (local activation) or on authenticated status (gated activation). Each contact offers $K$ message slots, an attempt has a transmission budget $B$ and a candidate lifetime of $L$ contacts, each message is lost with probability $p$ (independently, or through a two-state Gilbert-Elliott channel as a sensitivity probe), and the spacecraft restarts at a contact boundary with probability $r$ under one of three persistence levels (volatile, commit, journal). Attempts end in SUCCESS, INDETERMINATE, EXPIRED\_OLD, DIVERGED\_S\_AHEAD, or DIVERGED\_G\_AHEAD. Multi-attempt runs over a horizon of $H$ contacts use three policies: retry expired attempts only, additionally restart indeterminate attempts, or resume verification from the step reached; the last is an assumption outside the standards and is reported as a sensitivity. After divergence, a Recovery-association re-bootstrap follows a detection delay. A skipped-key cache desynchronization mechanism is analyzed separately and is not coupled to the exchange.

All probabilities are computed exactly in rational arithmetic by dynamic programming. The solver is cross-checked against an independent exhaustive enumerator, closed-form cases, Monte Carlo agreement, and mutation tests, and the suite of 99 tests passes natively and in a container. A TLC reachability check on the support is reported for transparency; it is same-author and its specification does not cover the resume policy or the Gilbert-Elliott channel.

### II.2 Parameter grid and anchors

The grid is the full factorial of $p \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$, $K \in \{2,3,4,6\}$, $B \in \{4,6,8,12\}$, $L \in \{1,\dots,4\}$, $r \in \{0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1\}$, three persistence levels, and two activation modes (7,680 configurations), crossed with $H \in \{4,8,20\}$, three policies, and detection delay in $\{1,3,5\}$ contacts (207,360 points).

No open measured TT\&C uplink-loss dataset was found, so parameters are anchored to proxies, each labelled by what it is. Observation timing comes from the SatNOGS windows used in an earlier study (476 scheduled observation windows, 20 satellite-station pairs, median duration 277 s); these are scheduled downlink observation windows, not contacts and not loss [@satnogs_api_2026]. Burst-level loss behavior comes from an 11-day consumer Starlink ping dataset [@borgianni_starlink_ping]: mean packet loss is 2.16\%, and a lossy 10-second burst is followed by another lossy burst 13.7\% of the time versus 7.8\% after a clean burst. This is broadband, not TT\&C. Published broadband ranges bracket the grid: baseline loss up to 2\% and storm-time uplink loss of up to 60\% at one site for limited periods [@basak_cosmicdancepro], and mean loss near 1\% with 22--25\% of samples showing some loss in a longitudinal dataset [@laniewski_wetlinks]. Orbital geometry gives modeled (not measured) pass durations of roughly 4--17 minutes at most for altitudes of 400--1200 km. The Gilbert-Elliott cases use a matched-stationary rule fixed before any run: a lossless good state, a fully lossy bad state, and a mean bad run of 2 or 4 slots at the same stationary loss as the independent case. Values $p = 0.3$ and $0.5$ are stress points beyond clear-sky broadband loss; their upper end is comparable to the worst broadband uplink loss reported during the 2024 solar storms and is not measured TT\&C loss.

### II.3 Pre-registration and disclosures

Hypotheses H-E1 to H-E5, the grid, and the policy set were frozen before any confirmatory run (tag \texttt{study-e-prereg-v1}, commit 93636b1). The confirmatory runner was added afterwards under a disclosed amendment (tag \texttt{study-e-analysis-script-v1}); it implements only the frozen hypotheses, and operationalizations of ambiguous wording were declared before the run. A small test grid run before the confirmatory run had revealed witnesses against H-E2b; the hypothesis was not changed. Starlink statistics were inspected before the Gilbert-Elliott rule was written, so the anchors are not blind. An erratum records that the frozen text cited only the baseline loss from the storm-analysis paper.

**Table 3. Study E hypotheses and outcomes over the full grid (exact arithmetic).**

| Hypothesis | Outcome |
|---|---|
| H-E2a: SUCCESS monotone in $B$, $K$, $L$, $H$ and non-increasing in $p$ | Holds for $B$, $L$, $H$ (no violations). Fails for $K$ (31 of 5,760 pairs) and $p$ (22 of 6,144); every violation has $r>0$ and non-journal persistence; largest changes 0.077 and 0.013. |
| H-E2b: more durable persistence never raises ground-ahead divergence (gated) | Falsified: 288 of 2,560 comparisons, all commit-to-journal, rise up to 0.082. |
| H-E2c: journal invariant to $r$; volatile divergence non-decreasing in $r$ | Journal invariance holds (0 of 1,920 violations). Volatile monotonicity fails in 447 of 1,920 pairs, with decreases up to 0.041. |
| H-E3: retry cannot absorb INDETERMINATE mass; resumable completion is the lever | Holds at all 5,520 configurations with INDETERMINATE mass: raising $H$ from 4 to 20 gains less than that mass, and resumable verification gains more than raising $H$. |
| H-E4: divergence almost always recoverable under a static Recovery SA | Median recovery fraction is about 1, but only 89.8\% of points (83.2\% under restart-indeterminate) reach the declared 0.95 threshold; minimum 0.58. Detection delay matters at $H=20$ mainly for $p\geq 0.3$ (25\% of points at 0.3, 61\% at 0.5; 5\% at 0.05) and strongly at $H=4$ (range up to 0.97). |
| H-E5: bursty loss at equal stationary loss changes outcomes in no fixed direction | Supported: bursty higher in 4,664 comparisons, lower in 3,112, equal in 7,584; maximum difference 0.317. |

### II.4 Interpretation

Completion, not repetition, is the lever: automatic retry of expired attempts adds little because the dominant failure is an exchange that ends indeterminate, and resumable verification closes most of that gap, subject to its being an assumption outside the standards. The falsified hypotheses are informative. In this model, durable persistence preserves progress that non-durable schemes reset, so under tight budgets more durable attempts end where ground has activated and the spacecraft has not; and extra slots or fewer losses can lower success when restarts can strike mid-exchange under non-durable persistence. These effects are consequences of the model's rules, not claims about real links; they warn that more durable and more reliable-link do not compose monotonically. Bursty loss can help or hurt, so a single independent-loss figure is not a safe planning assumption.

### II.5 Limitations specific to Study E

The six-message abstraction is not flight software and has not been independently reviewed by a second person. Parameters are anchored to proxies, none a measured TT\&C uplink. The Gilbert-Elliott channel is a sensitivity probe, not a calibrated channel. Recovery-association and resume-policy parameters are assumptions. Skipped-key desynchronization is not coupled to the exchange. The TLA+ check is same-author, omits the resume policy and the Gilbert-Elliott channel, and its tool jar digest was not compared to the publisher's. SatNOGS records are first-page only and not representative. Reproduction: the table digest \texttt{dfed99fa\dots a47d} was reproduced on macOS and in a second, separately built Linux container (same author); code, tests, TLA+ files, results, and pre-registration are in the repository at the cited tags.
