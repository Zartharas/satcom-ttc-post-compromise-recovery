Fresh key establishment does not by itself restore trusted telemetry, tracking, and command (TT&C) operation after compromise when intermittent contact, message loss, and endpoint state loss leave ground and spacecraft in different recovery states. Part I studies this problem in a deterministic software model with three baseline abstractions and a bounded resynchronization controller (T1), using four matched families, 40 deterministic T1 schedules, a fixed 100-schedule mixed-fault population, a 3 x 3 sensitivity grid (108 executions), and bounded TLA+/Python comparisons. The matched families showed categorical parity, not superiority. Of 31 deterministic fault cells, 25 succeeded, four were indeterminate, one expired, and one produced an unsafe spacecraft-ahead state. Single drops were absorbed by retries, but retransmission could not repair destroyed endpoint state; raising the transmission budget from two to three increased verification-complete executions from 5/12 to 11/12, with no further benefit from a fourth. Part II computes outcomes of a six-message activation-and-verification exchange exactly over 7,680 configurations and 207,360 multi-attempt configurations, with hypotheses frozen in the repository before the confirmatory run. Plain retries could not clear indeterminate outcomes, whereas resumable verification, an assumption outside the standards, could; bursty loss changed outcomes in both directions; and several expected monotonicities failed, with recovery reaching the declared 0.95 level in 83-90% of configurations rather than almost always. These are properties of the abstraction, not measured link behavior, and loss values are stress parameters, not measured TT&C uplink loss. Neither part claims conformance. The results support separating candidate state, activation, verification evidence, and persistent recovery state.
