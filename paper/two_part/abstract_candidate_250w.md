Fresh key establishment alone does not restore trusted satellite telemetry, tracking, and command operation after compromise when intermittent contact, message loss, and endpoint state loss leave ground and spacecraft in different states. Part I studies this deterministically, with three baseline abstractions and a resynchronization controller: four matched families, 40 controller schedules, a 100-schedule mixed population, a 3 x 3 sensitivity grid, and TLA+/Python comparisons. Baselines appear in matched families (two inject no fault, so parity checks consistency only) and a supplementary grid where retransmission absorbed isolated loss. Of 31 single-fault cells, 25 succeeded, four were indeterminate, and two ended unsafe, with a compromised key still active; so did all eight retry-exhaustion schedules. Retries absorbed single drops but not lost verification evidence or destroyed endpoint state; on twelve designed schedules, a third transmission raised verification-complete executions from 5/12 to 11/12. The mixed population produced 74 successes, but only 77 of 191 scheduled faults were reached, counts are descriptive. Part II computes a six-message activation-and-verification exchange exactly over 7,680 configurations and 207,360 multi-attempt configurations, with hypotheses frozen beforehand. Plain retries could not clear indeterminate outcomes; resumable verification, an assumption outside the standards, could; bursty loss changed outcomes in both directions; several expected monotonicities failed, and recovery reached the 0.95 level in 83-90% of divergent configurations, by retry policy. These are abstraction properties, not link measurements. Neither part claims CCSDS conformance. The results support separating candidate state, activation, verification evidence, and persistent recovery state.
