Fresh key establishment does not by itself restore trusted satellite telemetry, tracking, and command operation after compromise when intermittent contact, message loss, and endpoint state loss leave ground and spacecraft in different recovery states. Part I studies this problem in a deterministic software model with three baseline abstractions and a bounded resynchronization controller: four matched families, 40 deterministic controller schedules, a fixed 100-schedule mixed-fault population, a 3 x 3 sensitivity grid, and bounded TLA+/Python comparisons. The matched families showed categorical parity, not superiority. Of 31 deterministic fault cells, 25 succeeded, four were indeterminate, one expired, and one produced an unsafe spacecraft-ahead state. Retries absorbed single drops but could not repair destroyed endpoint state; raising the transmission budget from two to three increased verification-complete executions from 5/12 to 11/12. The mixed population produced 74 successes, but only 77 of 191 scheduled faults were reached, so those counts are descriptive, not reliability estimates. Part II computes a six-message activation-and-verification exchange exactly over 7,680 configurations and 207,360 multi-attempt configurations, with hypotheses frozen in the repository before the confirmatory run. Plain retries could not clear indeterminate outcomes, whereas resumable verification, an assumption outside the standards, could; bursty loss changed outcomes in both directions; several expected monotonicities failed, and recovery reached the declared 0.95 level in 83-90% of configurations, rather than almost always. These are properties of the abstraction, not measured link behavior, and loss values are stress parameters. Neither claims conformance. The results support separating candidate state, activation, verification evidence, and persistent recovery state.
